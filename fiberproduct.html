Research overview

Generalized Fiber Products of Elliptic Surfaces

1. The geometric object

Let \(\pi_1:\mathcal E^{(1)}\to\mathbb P^1_t\) and \(\pi_2:\mathcal E^{(2)}\to\mathbb P^1_s\) be elliptic surfaces over \(\mathbb Q\). Their generic fibres are elliptic curves \(E^{(1)}_t\) and \(E^{(2)}_s\). The two surfaces are coupled by a projective linear change of the base parameter.

\[ M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad ad-bc\ne0, \qquad \phi_M(t)=\frac{at+b}{ct+d}. \] \[ X_M :=\mathcal E^{(1)} \times_{\mathbb P^1_t}\phi_M^*\mathcal E^{(2)} \;\cong\; \mathcal E^{(1)}_t \times_{\mathbb P^1_t} \mathcal E^{(2)}_{\frac{at+b}{ct+d}}. \]

The matrix is understood projectively: multiplying all four entries by the same nonzero rational number does not change \(\phi_M\). The sweep stores a primitive integral representative, modulo simultaneous sign, and discards singular matrices with \(ad-bc=0\). The generic fibre of \(X_M\to\mathbb P^1_t\) is \(E^{(1)}_t\times E^{(2)}_{\phi_M(t)}\), an abelian surface whenever both elliptic fibres are smooth. Thus the total fibre product is a three-dimensional variety.

The map \(\phi_M\) changes which fibre of the second elliptic surface is paired with a given fibre of the first. It can align, separate, or permute distinguished singular-fibre parameters. This is why the Möbius transformation is part of the arithmetic object, rather than merely a change of notation.

2. Families used by the sweep

The family registry contains plane-cubic pencils and elliptic Weierstrass models. A plane pencil is represented schematically by \[ P(x,y,z)-tQ(x,y,z)=0 \quad\text{in }\mathbb P^2. \] Its fibre count is the number of projective solutions over \(\mathbb F_p\).

Weierstrass families are encoded in the form \[ y^2+a_1(t)xy+a_3(t)y =x^3+a_2(t)x^2+a_4(t)x+a_6(t). \] The kernel counts affine solutions and adds the zero-section point; it also treats the fibre at \(t=\infty\) using the leading coefficients after homogenization.

The packaged models include Beauville and Hulek--Verrill pencils, W/Herfurtner families, the \(J\)-family, and normalized Miranda--Persson / Jarvis--Lang--Ricks (MP/JLR) Weierstrass surfaces. For example, the normalized MP/JLR \(X_{321}\) model is \[ y^2+4txy=x^3+4t^3x. \]

3. How the Möbius sweep is generated

The program searches ordered pairs of family keys. For each pair it generates primitive matrices \(M=(a,b,c,d)\) with nonzero determinant and bounded coefficients. The default curated space starts with standard maps such as \(t\), \(1/t\), \(1-t\), \(t\pm1\), and simple fractional-linear maps; it then adds maps determined by the two families' distinguished singular-fibre values, followed by a low-height coefficient box.

The expanded strategy adds arithmetic relation grids and a broader coefficient pool, including maps with all four entries nonzero. The default coefficient bound is \(100\); the documented default curated cap is \(10{,}000\) matrices per ordered pair, while the expanded multiplier is \(30\). This is a structured search, not an exhaustive enumeration of all of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)\).

Fibre counts are cached by family and prime. Search progress is recorded separately for each ordered family pair, so a later run can resume without repeating an already-tested canonical matrix. Numba JIT kernels batch the residue calculation over primes.

4. Finite-field point counting

For a prime \(p\), let \(n_{i,p}(u)=\#\mathcal E^{(i)}_u(\mathbb F_p)\) denote the number of points on the fibre of the \(i\)-th elliptic surface over \(u\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)\). The base has \(p+1\) rational points: \(p\) finite parameters and the point at infinity. The fibre-product count is obtained by multiplying the two fibre counts above each base point and summing:

\[ A_p(M) =\#X_M(\mathbb F_p) =\sum_{u\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)} n_{1,p}(u)\, n_{2,p}\!\left(\phi_M(u)\right). \] \[ \mathrm{PC}_p=A_p(M),\qquad \mathrm{PC}_p\bmod p=A_p(M)\bmod p. \]

The configured sweep and the expanded tables use primes \(5\le p\le97\), with an initial screening range \(5\le p\le59\). A prime dividing \(\det(M)\) is also skipped: modulo such a prime the matrix no longer defines an automorphism of \(\mathbb P^1\). A dash in a form-trace row means that the corresponding trace is absent from the bundled database.

5. Comparison with classical modular and paramodular forms

The sweep uses the fixed point-count transform \[ v_p(M)=1-A_p(M)\pmod p, \] and compares it with the Hecke trace \(a_p(f)\) of a candidate form: \[ v_p(M)\equiv a_p(f)\pmod p \quad\text{or}\quad v_p(M)\equiv -a_p(f)\pmod p. \] In other words, the default matcher is sign-insensitive at each prime. It first screens candidates using \(p\le59\) and at least \(12\) signed matches, then performs the full comparison through \(p\le97\), requiring at least \(18\) signed matches and at least \(18\) valid primes.

Before matching, the program groups forms into sign-insensitive minimal-twist classes using their prime-trace absolute-value patterns. It compares against a minimal representative and then records the best exact member, its level, and the observed plus/minus/zero twist signature. The first column of the result tables is the minimal-twist representative, not necessarily the exact member that attained the largest unsigned score.

6. How the two result tables are constructed

  1. Candidate records are imported from the sweep database. The strict table builder uses the default signed-score threshold \(20\); this is stricter than the full-search acceptance threshold \(18\). The three compact isotrivial MP/JLR models \(X_{22}\), \(X_{33}\), and \(X_{44}\) are excluded from the strict structural tables, although they remain supported by the point-counting kernel.
  2. Exact duplicates are reduced by raw ordered family pair, primitive Möbius matrix, and minimal-twist label. The best-scoring record is retained.
  3. Family base coordinates are changed to the selected orbit representatives. Finite rational base automorphisms are used to form double-coset classes. Reversing the two factors and replacing \(M\) by \(M^{-1}\) is also identified, so the structural key is undirected.
  4. The builder groups by minimal-twist form and undirected geometric key, then selects a compact representative (favoring a small coefficient norm and, where possible, the identity). The current packaged snapshots contain \(171\) classical and \(125\) paramodular finite rows, with no infinite rows.
  5. The family-equation list is generated from the family registry. Its final column records the finite rational base automorphisms used in the structural quotient; it is not the full Möbius search space.

7. Reading an expanded row and predicting the twist

Each leftmost chevron opens a five-row, prime-by-prime table. The first column explains the row; the remaining columns list every prime \(2\le p\le97\). Point Counts is the exact integer \(A_p(M)\), PC mod p is its residue, Form Traces is \(a_p(f)\), and FT mod p is the corresponding residue.

The twist summary is inferred from the difference between the third and fifth data rows after applying the sweep's fixed \(1-A\) transform. At primes where the trace is nonzero modulo \(p\), define \(\varepsilon_p\in\{\pm1\}\) by \[ 1-A_p(M)\equiv\varepsilon_p a_p(f)\pmod p. \] The page then tests whether this sign pattern agrees with a quadratic character \[ \varepsilon_p=\chi_D(p)=\left(\frac{D}{p}\right), \] where \(D\) is a fundamental discriminant. The displayed prediction is a bounded post-processing fit over \(|D|\le5000\); if several discriminants fit because too few nonzero traces are available, the panel says so. A residual mismatch is displayed explicitly.

Interpretation and limitations. These are finite-field fingerprints and candidate correspondences, not a proof of modularity or paramodularity. The counting kernel counts the fibres of the supplied models, including singular fibres as they occur in those models; it does not resolve singularities or construct a crepant resolution. In particular, the geometric interpretation of additive fibres and the effect of a resolution require separate mathematical verification. A quadratic character fit through \(p\le97\) is evidence for a predicted twist, not a proof of an identity of \(L\)-functions.
